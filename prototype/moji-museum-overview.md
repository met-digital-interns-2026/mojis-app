# Moji Museum

## What is it?

Moji Museum is a fun, interactive app for visitors at The Metropolitan Museum of Art. It lets people react to artwork using emojis and share their thoughts with other visitors — kind of like social media, but for art.

## How it works

### Scanning artwork

When you're standing in front of a piece of art at The Met, open the app and tap the Scan button. Point your phone at the artwork and the app will recognize what you're looking at. Once it finds a match, you'll see the artwork's name, artist, year, and which gallery it's in. From there, tap "View Details & React" to go to the full artwork page.

### Reacting with emojis

This is the heart of the app. Instead of just "liking" something, you pick an emoji that matches how the art makes you feel — and then you choose *how much* you feel it. There are 8 emotion categories to pick from: Sad, Disgusted, Confused, Love, Funny, Scary, Mind-blowing, and Angry.

Each category has 6 levels of intensity. For example, if a painting makes you sad, you tap the Sad emoji and then pick from a scale that goes from slightly sad (😕) all the way to full-on crying (😭). It's a way more expressive way to react than just hitting a heart button.

You pick one reaction per artwork.

### Comments and conversations

Below the emoji reactions, there's a comments section where people can share their thoughts in speech bubbles. You can see what other visitors said about the artwork, like their comments with a heart, and reply to start a conversation. Your chosen emoji shows up next to your comment so people can see how you reacted.

### The homepage

The homepage shows the most reacted artworks across different categories — like "Saddest," "Most Loved," "Most Shocking," "Most Puzzling," and "Most Beautiful." Each card shows the artwork, how many reactions it has, and comments from other visitors. There's also a Trending section that shows which artworks are getting reactions right now.

### Art detail page

When you tap on an artwork (either from scanning or from the homepage), you get the full detail page. This includes a large image of the artwork, info about the artist and where to find it in the museum, a collapsible "About This Work" section with background info, the emoji intensity picker, all the comments and replies, and related artworks from the same exhibition.

## The vibe

The app has a fun, playful feel — bright colors, rounded shapes, and smooth animations. The scan page has a dark, camera-like look with a cool scanning animation. The homepage is warm and inviting. Everything is designed to work on your phone while you're walking through the museum.
